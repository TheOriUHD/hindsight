Press a key. Keep the last minute.

Hindsight records your screen in the background and saves the moment you just had as an MP4. It runs on your GPU, stays out of your game, and never asks you to sign in.

Library. Every clip in one folder, grouped by day and by game. Hover a thumbnail to scrub through it before you open it.

It's already recording when something happens.

There's no record button to remember. Hindsight keeps a rolling buffer of the last seconds, so the clip exists before you knew you wanted it.

  • Always on, mostly idle. The screen is captured and compressed on the graphics card. What's kept in memory is already a video, not raw frames.
  • One key saves it. F8 by default, or any combo or mouse side button. It works while a fullscreen game has focus and never takes the key away from the game.
  • Written, not re-encoded. The popup confirms the moment you press the key. Saving copies the already-compressed data into an MP4 in one pass, at full quality: a 30-second 4K clip is usually on disk well under a second later.

A small card slides out of the screen edge when the clip is saved, with a short sound. It never takes focus and doesn't show up in your recordings.

Built so you can leave it running.

Frames never leave the GPU. The video encoder on your graphics card (NVENC on NVIDIA, AMF on AMD) does the work, not the cores your game is using. Measured on an RTX 5090 at 4K:

Out of the game process

Hindsight uses Windows' own screen capture. Nothing is injected into the game, so it doesn't trip anti-cheat.

Nothing heavy in the background

The recorder is a small native process with no browser engine inside. The library window is separate and fully closes when you close it.

Smooth, not just fast

Clips come out at a constant frame rate with audio locked to the same clock, so editors and Discord play them without stutter or drift.

Everything after the moment, too.

Find the clip, cut it to the part that matters, and get it under the upload limit, without opening an editor.

Instant scrubbing

Every clip carries a small preview track, so dragging along the timeline shows the frame under your cursor immediately, even for 4K at 240 fps.

Trim in place

Set in and out points and save a copy. Instant at the nearest keyframe, or frame-accurate with a quick re-encode on the GPU.

Export to a size

Pick 10, 25 or 50 MB and Hindsight picks the bitrate and resolution so the file fits, as H.264 that plays everywhere.

Separate audio tracks

Game and microphone are mixed for sharing and also kept on their own tracks, so you can fix levels later.

Best codec available

AV1 where your card supports it, then HEVC, then H.264. Up to 240 fps, native resolution or scaled down.

Your hotkey

Any key combination or a mouse side button. Pause from the tray icon when you don't want anything kept.

Your clips stay on your PC.

There is no Hindsight account and no cloud library. Clips are ordinary MP4 files in your Videos folder.

  • No sign-in, no cloud library, no ads, no subscription.
  • No telemetry or analytics. Logs stay in a local folder and only leave it in a problem report you choose to send.
  • Update checks are the only automatic network request, and you can turn them off.
  • The replay buffer lives in memory and is gone when you quit. Only clips you save are written to disk.
  • Pause recording from the tray, and the hotkey does nothing until you resume.

Set up in a minute.

  1. Install

    Run the installer. It installs for your user only and doesn't need admin rights. Windows builds aren't code-signed yet, so SmartScreen asks you to confirm the first launch.

  2. Play

    Hindsight starts in the tray and records your main display at 60 fps by default. Change resolution, frame rate and clip length in Settings.

  3. Press F8

    The last minute is saved to Videos\Hindsight, named after the game you were playing.

Questions

Something missing? Open an issue on GitHub.

Is it safe with anti-cheat?

Hindsight doesn't inject anything into games or hook their rendering. It uses the same Windows screen capture any recording or streaming tool can use, and reads your keyboard through Windows' raw input, which doesn't interfere with the game.

Does it cost anything?

No. Hindsight is free for personal and other noncommercial use, with no paid tier and nothing locked. The source is available under the PolyForm Noncommercial license; commercial use needs a separate license.

How much does it slow my game down?

Very little. Encoding runs on the dedicated video engine of your graphics card, and the recorder itself uses a few percent of one CPU core. At very high resolutions and frame rates it automatically uses a faster encoder setting so it keeps up.

How much memory does it use?

The replay buffer holds compressed video, so a minute of 1080p at 60 fps is around 100 MB and a minute of 4K is a few hundred MB. Settings shows the most it can use with your choices. The graphics driver also reserves memory for the encoder, more at 4K.

Why don't I see the "Clip saved" popup in some games?

Games in exclusive fullscreen draw over everything, including the popup. Most games now use borderless or flip-model fullscreen, where it shows. The save sound plays either way.

Where are my clips?

In Videos\Hindsight by default, as normal MP4 files you can open, move or share. You can change the folder in Settings.

Which games and apps does it record?

It records a whole display: whatever is on the screen you choose, game or not. Clips are named after the window that was in front when you pressed the hotkey.

Why does Windows warn about the installer?

The installer isn't code-signed yet, so SmartScreen doesn't recognise it. Choose More info, then Run anyway. The source and the build steps are on GitHub if you'd rather build it yourself.
